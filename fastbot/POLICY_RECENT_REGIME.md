# POLICY: the recent-regime track (agreed 2026-10-04)

I am not a financial adviser. This is a measurement, not a recommendation.

**Why.** Markets have changed a lot since about 2013 (electronic and algorithmic trading, commission-free retail apps, zero-day options, machine-readable filings, AI). Our record shows effects that died after reforms (gold fix 2015, Kurov FOMC drift after 2015) and effects that only exist recently (v56 breadth, v36 noise band). Requiring every era since 2005 to pass can throw away something that is real *now*.

**The risk on the other side.** Choosing "since 2013" after seeing which tests look good is hindsight: out of ~130 tests, several will look consistent over ten years by chance alone. Recent-only windows are also short, so they have less power. And today's lesson: two "recent-only" winners (PEAD3, the first-day move) were bugs.

**The rule.**
1. The main gates stay as they are (every era), so a PASS still means robust.
2. A result that FAILS the early era but is positive in **every** sub-era since 2013, has had the look-ahead audit, and costs nothing to record, goes onto a **forward record** with its own frozen pre-registration and a fixed verdict date.
3. Nothing on this track is traded with real money until its forward verdict PASSES.
4. New tests may pre-register a 2013+ primary window **before** seeing data, with the earlier years reported only. That is honest; choosing the window afterwards is not.

**On the track now:** FORWARD_v36 (NAS100 noise band: 2005–12 net −2.1 bp, 2013–19 +3.0, 2020–26 +2.9) · FORWARD_SMALLCAP1 (PEAD small caps) · FORWARD_BREADTH1 (v56 breadth). Checked and **not** qualifying: TOM v46 (2013–19 negative), INTERNALS v54 (only 2022+), TIMELINE v37 (shrinking era by era), GEX v42 H1 (positive in all eras already; failed on t), REACTIVE (negative since 2013).
