## The question

Arms A to F were run cold: 21 market names, a data description, one
sentence. ARM G gets the opposite — everything this programme has learned
in 184 pre-registrations and 162 results.

**Does accumulated negative knowledge produce a better strategy, or just a
better-informed failure?**

## Why this is worth doing now rather than earlier

Three things exist today that did not when this was first sketched:

1. **The gate results for six blind arms** — a real control group, built
   and scored, costing nothing extra.
2. **The holding-period finding** — measured by our gates: every arm found
   a positive gross edge and only the short-hold ones kept it after costs.
3. **Measured spreads** — not assumed. Index spreads turned out to be half
   what a model guessed; soft commodities nearly double.

None of that was available to arms A-F. All of it is available to ARM G.

**And the programme had already found the same thing independently.**
`PROGRAMME.md` section 12b, written 2026-10-10: *"Spread-only accounting is
not adequate for overnight holds."* Our six blind arms did not know it; our
gates rediscovered it a month later. That convergence is itself worth a
beat in the episode.

## The honest limitation

ARM G cannot be compared to arms A-F as a controlled arm. It is not
sealed, by design. What it can be compared on is narrow and specific:
**what it proposes**, and **whether it avoids what is closed**. Not
whether its strategy is better, which is confounded by everything it was
told.

## Its only real out-of-sample test

Our 2005-2026 data has now been used by 184 pre-registrations, six arms
and one gate engine. For ARM G it is not out-of-sample at any remove.
The twelve-month forward window in `FORWARD_REGISTRATION.md` is the only
honest test of anything it produces, and that is not a caveat — it is the
reason to run it.
